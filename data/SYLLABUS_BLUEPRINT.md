## 2. The 5-subject structure (this is fixed — confirmed on the actual paper, not just the syllabus PDF)

| # | Section | Questions | Split |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Child Development & Pedagogy (CDP) | 30 | — |
| 2 | Language I — **Urdu** | 30 | 24 content + 6 pedagogy-of-language |

## 3. What WILL come — subject by subject (verified from two real exam sittings, Oct 2024 and Dec 2025)

### Child Development & Pedagogy (30Q)
Purely theoretical, no numericals. Recurring, stable topics across both years checked:
- **Developmental psychology**: Piaget (stages, conservation, egocentrism), Vygotsky (ZPD, higher mental processes, scaffolding), Kohlberg (moral development stages), Erikson (psychosocial stages), Freud (psychosexual stages).
- **Learning theories**: Thorndike, Gestalt/configuration theory, theory of identical elements (Bagley), formal discipline, classical/operant conditioning, types of learning (direct/indirect/problem-solving/principles).
- **Individual differences & intelligence**: multiple intelligences (Gardner-style categories — social, spatial, intra-personal, etc.), creativity vs intuition vs critical thinking, aptitude tests (DAT), divergent/convergent thinking.
- **Personality & assessment tools**: Sheldon, Jung, Kretschmer, Spranger (theorist–nationality pairs are a recurring question format), projective tests (Rorschach ink-blot, TAT), inventories (MMPI, Bell's Adjustment Inventory).
- **Memory & forgetting**: repression, disuse, retroactive/proactive interference, methods of measuring retention.
- **Policy & current education law/schemes** — this category is *the* highest-yield "read the news" area: **RTE Act 2009** (specific sections), **NEP 2020** (exact approval date, structure), **NCF**, **NDEAR**, ICT-in-education terms (file formats, software categories), ABC card (Academic Bank of Credits).
- **Inclusive/special education**: learning disabilities (dyslexia, dysgraphia, dyscalculia), parenting styles, giftedness, window charts and other SEN teaching aids.
- **Classroom & motivation theory**: Maslow-style needs, frustration-aggression (Dolard), stress theory (Kurt Lewin), Bloom's taxonomy domains.

### Language I — Urdu (30Q: 24 content + 6 pedagogy)
This is the section most candidates under-prepare because they assume "primary-level Urdu is easy." The real papers show otherwise:
- **Grammar (سنجیدہ گرامر)**: مترادف (synonyms), ضد (antonyms), جمع (plurals), سابقہ/لاحقہ (prefix/suffix word-formation), ضمیر کی اقسام (pronoun types), فعل کی اقسام, درست املا (correct spelling — a recurring, high-difficulty format), جملے کا نحوی تجزیہ (parts of a sentence — فاعل/مفعول/فعل).
- **Literature & literary history**: named poets and their pen-names/takhallus (e.g., Jigar Muradabadi, Shibli Nomani, Baiqal Ehsahi), poetic forms (رباعی، دوہا، مرثیہ، نظم), identifying figures of speech in Urdu couplets (تشبیہ، استعارہ، تجنیس، تضاد، مبالغہ), specific poem/author matching.
- **Language origin & linguistics theory** (this repeats every year): Prakrit/Sanskrit/Pali debate, "Bow-Wow" and other origin-of-language theories, Hindavi/Devanagari history.
- **Cultural & religious general knowledge woven into the language paper**: Sufi saints (Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti and lineage), the Prophet's names and family, Deccan-Urdu regional history (Begum Hazrat Mahal), and similar — this is **not optional side content**, it appears as core, scored MCQs.
- **Pedagogy of Urdu (6Q)**: LSRW skill order (listening→speaking→reading→writing is the standard teaching sequence tested), curriculum-component questions, teaching-method philosophy.

### Language II — English (30Q: 24 content + 6 pedagogy)
- **Vocabulary & usage**: synonym/antonym in context, idioms (very common — "beat about the bush," "make no bones"), correct spelling, word-formation (suffixes like -able/-ible), parts of speech identification.
- **Grammar**: tense/subject-verb agreement, articles, prepositions, active↔passive, direct↔indirect speech, sentence types (simple/complex/compound), clause identification, modals.
- **Reading comprehension**: one short prose passage + one short poetry extract per paper, each with 1 inference-style question.
- **Pedagogy of English (6Q)**: testing techniques (dictation for writing skills, controlled conversation for speaking, etc.), historical education-policy facts stated in English (Wood's Dispatch 1854), syllable counting, Bloom's taxonomy applied to language testing.

### Mathematics (30Q: 24 content + 6 pedagogy)
- **Content is NOT capped at Class 5 level** — real questions include quadratic-style factorisation, algebraic identities (x²+y² from xy and a symmetric equation), Euclid's postulates, decagon diagonals, mensuration (cylinders, cuboids, composite floor areas), probability, statistics (median formula components), ratio & proportion chains (A:B:C), profit/loss %, twin primes, Pythagorean triplets — this is solid Class 6–10 command expected of the *teacher*, even though they'll teach Class 1–5.
- **Pedagogy of Maths (6Q)**: concrete-to-abstract teaching aids (Cuisenaire rods, Tangram), the sequence in which symbols/operations are introduced to Class 1–2 children, Bloom's taxonomy in maths question-setting, item-writing rules (completion-type items), textbook-philosophy quotes attributed to named educationists (Hall Quest, etc.).

### Environmental Studies (30Q: 24 content + 6 pedagogy)
The broadest section — genuinely interdisciplinary, drawing on:
- **Physics**: heat transfer modes, pressure, periscope mirrors, conductors/insulators.
- **Chemistry**: metals/non-metals properties, solutions (isotonic/hypertonic/hypotonic), combustion pollutants (SO₂ sources).
- **Biology**: symbiosis (lichens), cloning (Dolly the sheep — specific cell type), adaptation, food-testing experiments (iodine/CO₂/protein tests).
- **Agriculture**: mulching, intercropping, sowing practices.
- **History**: named battles (Tarain II), colonial policy (Doctrine of Lapse, Wood's Dispatch), social reformers (Savitribai Phule — state and dates).
- **Geography & civics**: population density comparisons by state/census year, factors affecting population distribution, Mandal Tahsildar office functions, comparative religion facts (Sikhism).
- **Current-ish GK folded into EVS**: e.g. end-year of a recent geopolitical event (Sri Lanka civil war, 2009).
- **Pedagogy of EVS (6Q)**: aims-vs-objectives distinctions, educationist quotes (Gandhiji, John Dewey, John Keats, Nicholas Wright — matching quotes to names is a recurring format), teaching methods (discussion/project/problem-solving/inquiry-based) matched to descriptive phrases.

## 4. What will **NOT** come — be precise about this, it saves study time

- **No negative marking** — never skip a question out of fear; guessing has zero downside.
- **No subjective/descriptive/essay answers anywhere.** Everything is 4-option MCQ, CBT-clicked. Don't prepare "answer writing."
- **No standalone General Knowledge / Current Affairs section.** GK-flavoured facts (Sri Lanka war, population census) appear *only* folded inside EVS, not as a separate section — don't buy a separate GK book for this exam.
- **No standalone Reasoning/Aptitude/Quantitative-Aptitude section.** Unlike many state/bank exams, AP TET Paper 1 has no puzzles, seating arrangement, syllogisms, or data-interpretation sets. (DAT/aptitude *theory* appears only as one CDP definition question, not as a skill section.)
- **No standalone Computer/IT section.** Digital-education terms (NDEAR, .docx, ABC card) show up as 1–2 embedded CDP pedagogy questions, not a dedicated ICT paper.
- **No Class VI–VIII subject content** (Science, Social Studies as separate subjects) — that is Paper 2 territory. Paper 1 candidates do not need Physics/Chemistry/Biology/History as separate subjects; only what's folded into the EVS section as described above.
- **No handwriting, interview, lesson-plan-demo, or viva component in Paper 1 itself** — Paper 1 is purely the eligibility CBT; teaching-demo/interview stages (if any) belong to a separate recruitment process (DSC), not TET.
- **No English-medium-only content for Urdu-medium candidates** — you get every CDP/Maths/EVS question in Urdu alongside English; you are never forced to read a section in English only (Language-II content itself is naturally English, by definition of what's being tested).

## 5. Year-over-year stability (why the 2025 paper is a reliable predictor for 2026)

Cross-checked CDP and Language-I Urdu questions from the **Oct 2024** sitting (two different shifts, SGT & Paper 1A, Urdu medium) against the **Dec 2025** sitting:
- Same theorist roster in CDP (Piaget/Vygotsky/Kohlberg/Erikson/Kretschmer/Jung/Sheldon/Spranger) — this list has not changed.
- Same Urdu-section shape: grammar (mutaraadif/zid/imla) + literary history/poets + Islamic-cultural general knowledge + pedagogy-of-language — identical mix, different specific items.
- Same "theorist quote → name the person" and "match the pair" question formats across both years, in every subject.
- No structural change in marks distribution, timing, or negative-marking policy between the two years, and the 2026 official notification confirms the same 150/150/150 structure.

**Conclusion: 2026 will very likely follow the identical blueprint above.** The only genuinely new-for-2026 risk area is current-events-style policy questions (NEP 2020 implementation updates, any newer NCF revision, newer digital-education schemes) — treat "policy & current scheme" as the one category worth actively refreshing right up to your exam date, everything else in this blueprint is stable.
